# Architecture

The engine uses an immutable contribution calendar, deterministic demo input, a GitHub GraphQL collector, five scenario planners and self-contained SVG renderers. Shared calendar helpers resolve intensity and board width; shared label and animation modules serialize consistent SVG geometry and timelines.

SVG output uses embedded declarative animation without JavaScript. GitHub image rendering compatibility and live API access require verification.

The collector accepts credentials from the environment through the CLI. The default demo requires no token.

GitOrigami animates six triangular SVG paper panels per active day. A deterministic active-cell wave schedules folding, flight and fade-out without waiting for empty columns. Each source cell is restored after its own flight. The CLI selects a renderer through a scenario registry. CI generates demo previews without credentials; the manual publishing workflow fetches live data once for the five public scenarios.

Publishing renders and XML-validates ten public assets from one calendar before uploading an artifact. A separate job with contents write permission updates the output branch without force-pushing. Generation uses read-only repository permissions and receives the contribution token only during the fetch step. Existing assets remain available if generation fails.

GitChess schedules a team of up to four attackers with queen, rook, bishop and knight movement. A breadth-first search plans unobstructed captures and preparatory moves, with a shared occupancy map preventing friendly collisions. The team takes turns. After every active day has been captured, attackers return to their starting squares before restoration. Reverse traversal is a deterministic fallback for obstructed return paths.

All five public scenarios share SMIL timeline serialization, retaining plateau boundaries while removing redundant interior keyframes and trailing numeric zeros. The registry drives batch generation and the composite action; selected themes and scenarios reuse one calendar fetch. No runtime speedup or browser-performance guarantee has been benchmarked yet.

GitChess uses contiguous, low-contrast sage squares and reserve dots of four radii. At most four upcoming targets are visible as level-coded dark chess pieces; all other contributions remain quiet dots. Source coordinates, metadata and activity levels are preserved.

GitBlast uses one persistent actor and a BFS route through free squares. The planner selects a reachable bomb site adjacent to a standing contribution brick, verifies a safe escape outside the blast cross, and schedules only one bomb at a time. Flames stop at the first intact brick on each ray. Cleared bricks remain passable until the hero returns home and the full calendar resets. All actor routes and blast crosses stay within the seven calendar rows. If no free 2x2 pocket exists, the minimum-occupancy pocket is opened before entry; affected contribution cells retain their metadata and restore at the loop boundary. This is deterministic SVG choreography, not an interactive game.

GitTanks uses breadth-first search on the contribution grid. One golden tank destroys bricks along clear cardinal firing lines of up to five cells. Waves of up to three silver enemies spawn only on reachable free squares within two Manhattan cells of a standing brick and pursue the hero using hunter, ambusher and flanker roles. No valid spawn means no new wave. Each pursuit tick reserves occupied and destination cells to prevent overlaps and head-on swaps; the hero continues clearing reachable bricks or fights visible enemies at range. Projectile travel time scales with distance; standing bricks and intervening tanks block firing lines. Bounded pursuit gives way to a deterministic interception fallback. The hero absorbs counterfire with a shield and clears every active day once. The calendar restores only after the hero returns home. The eagle remains decorative, and a full grid uses a top-edge entry square. The simulation is precomputed, not an interactive game.

All five public renderers share calendar geometry and SVG label rendering. Months are anchored at the exact first displayed week; overlapping boundary months occupy alternating header baselines without shifting columns. Mon, Wed and Fri are right-aligned and vertically centered on Sunday-first rows 1, 3 and 5. The shared width helper measures to the last cell edge before adding the right margin.

GitChess uses the shared month and weekday header. A restrained origin-and-destination highlight replaces separate capture flashes. Opposing contribution pieces remain stationary; this is a tactical visualization, not a full two-player chess engine.

GitInvaders schedules one persistent ship against a formation of original pixel-art aliens. Each active day maps to one alien, with its relative contribution level determining silhouette and color. The formation sways along a common horizontal timeline, holding its position during each column's engagement. Columns alternate between the edges of local eight-column sectors to avoid a monotonic sweep or repeated full-board traversals. Within each sector, repeated rounds revisit surviving columns. The lowest remaining alien fires first; the ship evades by 16 units except for every ninth engagement, when it absorbs a hit and flashes before counterattacking. At most two invaders are removed per visit, in bottom-up order. Missed shots end below the ship's lane. Laser positions, enemy visibility and cell restoration share the battle clock; no empty days generate invaders. At the loop boundary the ship and formation return home and all active cells restore. GitFactory and GitAbyss are retired from the public catalog; their implementation remains recoverable in Git history.

The shared layout module completes missing grid slots with static visual-only placeholders in all five renderers. Partial initial and final weeks preserve the seven-row board without creating Day records, dates, counts, labels or simulated activity. GitChess uses the matching checkerboard parity; other scenarios use their inactive-cell palette.

The shared compact layout uses 9-unit labels, month baselines at 10 and 20, and a grid top of 38. The second baseline and top clearance protect crowded month boundaries and GitTanks' dense-board entry. Footer titles are omitted; an optional signature reserves ten additional vertical units. Scenario-specific lower margins preserve sprite movement, the chess staging row, ship evasion, and eagle base. Origami flight centers are clamped below the month header. GitInvaders uses balanced per-round burst sizes and rotates round boundaries to avoid repeated columns when another remains available.

GitBlast adds original pixel-style hero and enemy sprites, muted stone masonry and contrasting dual-theme palettes. Up to three local enemy patrols per bomb encounter reserve distinct free routes outside every hero square for the encounter. Patrols caught at the blast endpoint disappear on impact; others withdraw after the flame clears. Enemy placement never changes the contribution data or the hero's safe route. If free space is insufficient, fewer or no enemies spawn. Enemy contact damage is not modeled.

Enemy deployment is staggered by at least 0.36 seconds in both GitBlast and GitTanks. GitBlast ghosts originate from a destroyed day or a neighboring free cell after the explosion clears; there is at most one ghost per destroyed source and three active ghosts at once. Precomputed routes reserve hero movement and other enemy paths, and surviving ghosts remain until loop reset. GitTanks deploys enemy tanks on separate simulation ticks before pursuit; per-enemy birth timestamps drive visibility and route validation.
